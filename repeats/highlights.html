Reading repeats blind across the disordered proteome

We hid every protein's name and annotations, asked a language model to read the raw sequence for anything that recurs, and kept only the copies that exist verbatim. It recovers known repeat families, finds short motifs scattered through disordered regions, and turns up a few things we can't yet name.

In short

Repeats in disordered regions are hard to find by alignment because copies are short, degenerate and scattered. Reading sequences the way a person would, scanning for anchored patterns rather than aligning them, picks them up alongside the classic structured arrays. Every result here survived a strict check: each reported copy has to appear, letter for letter, in the real sequence.

Method

Five steps, with the answers kept out of reach

The pipeline keeps the reading step honest. Scripts handle cohort selection, blinding and verification and contain no motif-finding logic; the model sees only an anonymous sequence.

01

Pick the cohort

Human proteins with ≥100 aa of disorder, from UniProt / MobiDB-lite regions or a TOP-IDP propensity window.

02

Blind

Strip names and annotations, shuffle to anonymous IDs, and keep the key in a separate place.

03

Read twice

One scan for long structural repeats, then an independent scan of disordered stretches for short anchored motifs (≥5 aa, ≥3 copies).

04

Verify verbatim

Each reported copy must occur exactly in the sequence. Coordinates are computed here; anything that doesn't match is dropped.

05

Unblind & compare

Only now attach identities, then check against UniProt features and AlphaFold models.
