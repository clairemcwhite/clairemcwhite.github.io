A blinded language-model screen for repeats in human disordered proteins

McWhite Lab, Department of Molecular and Cellular Biology, University of Arizona

Repeated motifs in intrinsically disordered regions are hard to find by alignment: the copies are short, differ from one another, and are spread out along the sequence. We gave a language model anonymized human protein sequences, with names and annotations removed, and asked it to describe any recurring pattern as a consensus together with the copies it was based on. A script then located each reported copy in the sequence and discarded any that could not be found. Set against UniProt modification sites and ELM motif annotations, the screen places known sites and motifs at a fixed position in the repeat unit, finds the same motifs in arrays that have not been curated, and turns up positional modification patterns with no description.

Method

Scripts handle cohort selection, blinding and coordinate lookup and contain no motif-finding logic. The model sees only an anonymous sequence.

1

Cohort

Human proteins with ≥100 aa of disorder, from UniProt / MobiDB-lite regions or a TOP-IDP propensity window.

2

Blinding

Names and annotations are removed and sequences get shuffled anonymous IDs. The key is kept separately.

3

Two-pass reading

One pass for long structural repeats, then an independent pass over disordered stretches for short anchored motifs (≥5 aa, usually ≥3 copies).

4

Locating copies

The model quotes each copy it reports. A script finds each quote in the sequence and records its coordinates; quotes that aren't in the sequence are dropped. Copies need not match each other.

5

Unblinding

Identities are attached only afterwards, then results are compared with UniProt features, ELM and AlphaFold models.
