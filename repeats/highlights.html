LLM-Guided Discovery of Protein Repeat Motifs in Intrinsically Disordered Regions

A systematic screen applying large language model reasoning to discover short linear motifs (SLiMs) and IDR motifs in human proteins — finding structured repeat architectures that traditional bioinformatics tools miss.

Approach

Traditional bioinformatics tools for repeat detection rely on exact or near-exact sequence similarity, making them blind to motifs that vary considerably between instances — the hallmark of biologically functional short linear motifs in intrinsically disordered regions.

We prompted a large language model (LLM) with protein sequences and asked it to reason about recurring structural or functional patterns, then generate a consensus notation describing each discovered motif. The LLM approach naturally handles the ambiguity and variation characteristic of real SLiMs, finding motifs like zinc-finger C₂H₂ variants, proline-rich repeats, and large helical repeats that share function but diverge in sequence.

All candidate motifs were manually curated and verified against known protein literature and AlphaFold structures before inclusion in this dataset. Full sequences, instance coordinates, and curation notes are available in the interactive curation tool.
